02and needs both

a and b is True only when both sides are True. If either one is False, the whole thing is False.

Example 4.2.4 President checks two requirements: you must be at least 35 and be a US citizen. Flip the two variables and watch which row of the truth table lights up. A truth table lists every possible combination of inputs, so you can see the whole rule at once.

03or needs at least one

a or b is True when at least one side is True. It’s only False when both sides are False.

Example 4.2.5 Wasting Time says you’re wasting time if you’re on TikTok or on Instagram. Its comment spells out the rule: true if one, or the other, or both.

Lab: 4.2.5 Wasting Time

English “or” is sloppier than Python’s. When a menu says “soup or salad,” it usually means one, not both. Python’s or always counts “both” as True. Compare the two truth tables: and and or agree when both inputs match, and disagree in the two middle rows, where exactly one input is True.

04Mixing operators

You can combine operators in one expression. When you do, Python has an order, just like multiplication before addition in math:

  1. Anything in parentheses first
  2. then not
  3. then and
  4. then or last

So True or False and False is True, because the and happens first. Use the lab to see each step. The highlighted part is what Python works out next.

05Parentheses & truth tables

  
Say a theater rule is: “You can go to the movies if you have money, and it’s either the weekend or a holiday.” These two lines look almost the same:


go = has_money and (is_weekend or is_holiday)   # what the rule says
go = has_money and is_weekend or is_holiday     # not the same!

  
Because and goes first, Python reads the second line as (has_money and is_weekend) or is_holiday. That lets you in on any holiday, even with no money. You won’t spot that by trying one or two values. You spot it by checking every combination, which is exactly what a truth table does.

06On the AP exam

  
The AP exam reference sheet has all three operators. They mean exactly the same thing as in Python, but they’re written in capitals.

  

    
IdeaPythonAP pseudocode

    
Oppositenot is_rainingNOT is_raining

    
Botha and ba AND b

    
At least onea or ba OR b

    
The valuesTrue, Falsetrue, false

  

  
The capitals are exactly backwards. Python writes operators in lowercase and values with a capital (not True). AP pseudocode writes operators in capitals and values in lowercase (NOT true). Writing AND in Python is a syntax error.

  
This is the AP topic Boolean Expressions. Exam questions often describe a rule in words and ask which expression matches it, or show two expressions and ask if they’re equivalent. The truth table machine in section 05 is the same check you’d do on paper.
